[[Walleye Pollock Fishery#Excluders]]
## What Qualifies as a Success

“In an evaluation of the earliest salmon excluder design that resulted in 12–20 % salmon escapement, 2–3 % of pollock loss was considered a justifiable tradeoff to queried pollock captains” (Yochum et al., 2021, p. 9)

## Testing Excluders

“A Reynolds Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) flow solver was applied to investigate the flow field in and around the initial excluder concept.” (Yochum et al., 2021, p. 3)

“The computational fluid dynamics simulations and flume tank testing expedited the development of the RT&F excluder by reasonably predicting performance at full scale and under commercial fishing conditions, including different tow speeds. Given the dynamic and cumbersome testing environment at sea, employing multiple tools to design fishing gear before testing at-sea can greatly benefit development” (Yochum et al., 2021, p. 8)

“In March 2019, we tested the modeled excluder design at 1:2 scale in an 8-m wide × 4-m deep × 22.25 m long flume tank at the Fisheries and Marine Institute of Memorial University of Newfoundland” (Yochum et al., 2021, p. 3)

“June 2019, during the ‘B’ season (summer/fall) Bering Sea pollock fishery, we tested a full-scale version of the RT&F excluder design (Fig. 4) that resulted from the fluid dynamics simulations and flume tank testing. T” (Yochum et al., 2021, p. 4)

“Pollock and salmon were observed using video from low-light cam­ eras (Sexton, https://www.thesextonco.com/) placed strategically in the excluder, with the field of view illuminated by integrated LED lighting” (Yochum et al., 2021, p. 5)
