Ended up spending far more than an hour on all of this :) 

Ultimately a "habitat" is an area in which creatures spend a disproportionate amount of time as compared to what one would expect from pure diffusion - i.e. information dropped into the area stays in the area. Literally then we want to optimize the retained information relative to the expected retained information across a series of contiguous groups. The problem with the ideas I've been playing around with today is that none of them actually optimize this. Just because an area accumulates information doesn't mean it does so generally from the whole area around it. Just because a place is sticky doesn't mean there are any fish nearby to stick to it (think valley). By identifying the basins of attraction and their boundaries we can start to sort out who lives where and what they do there. So what I'd like to do is optimize this whole thing. Probably using ant hill climbing or something like that. 

Tried a few different strategies for this none of them really worked. The map is extremely messy. Aggregations tend to just create a big clump 'cause information is just generally moving around. And then attempting optimization is weird 'cause the space is pretty jumpy and one needs to know where to start in the first place.
