## Otter trawls
### Otter trawl zones
#### Otter trawl zone 2
>By removing the top rope, fish that rise are caught less without necessarily  losing fish that don't. (Haddock vs flatfish for example)

-Kennelly 2021

>Raising the foot rope reduces bycatch of flatfish whilst often maintaining catch rates of target species. This also can reduce benthic infauna and debris in the catch.

-Kennelly 2021

>Semicircular spreading ground gear improved catching efficiency for  larger cod and haddock while reducing drag.

-Kennelly 2021

>Removing tickler chains reduces catchs of elasmobranchs without affecting targeted haddock, whiting or flatfish. This takes advantage of herding behavior.

-Kennelly 2021

>LEDs (especially at night) can help with bycatch

-Kennelly 2021

#### Otter trawl zone 3
>Increasing mesh size helps keep the fish you want and remove the fish you don't.

-Kennelly 2021

>Horizontal separator panels can help sort downwards oriented fish from  upwards rising ones.

-Kennelly 2021

>One can make "windows" of different shape or size mesh to allow certain kinds of  fish to escape.

-Kennelly 2021

#### Other
>Raising sweeps reduces bycatch of bottom dwelling species and optimizing the spread ratio can significantly help improve catches of target species.

-Kennelly 2021

>Reducing the circumference of the codend or length of extension can reduce bycatch.

-Kennelly 2021

>More flexible twine and single twine often improves selectivity.

-Kennelly 2021

>Simply replacing T0 with T45 mesh does wonders.

-Kennelly 2021

>Windows made of different kinds of mesh can be very useful here. You can also create windows to guide fish to those windows.

-Kennelly 2021

>Grids like the Sort-X and sort-V and flexigrids have been shown to be useful. However windows seem to be able to give the same bang  for significantly less hassle. However they remain quite popular

-Kennelly 2021

### Otter trawl modification testing
>Most common testing strategy (51%) is to cover the codend to capture escaping fish. The second most common strategy is to do alternate hauls  (22%). More often used for zone 1 - 3 modifications.

-Kennelly 2021

>People define the selectivity of modified gear on the basis  of selection range size or 50% size at selection.

-Kennelly 2021

>There's a need in these bycatch mitigation tests to improve the spatio-temporal coverage and replication of experiments.

-Kennelly 2021

>A great many things can influence selectivity including but not limited to temperature, fish condition, sea state, diel patterns, towing speed, haul back delay, catch weight, and water depth. Also included here would be the codend lengths twine diamters, netting materis, and so on.

-Kennelly 2021

>There's a need to standardize on measures of effectiveness as well as  how to communicate the effectively to stakeholders.

-Kennelly 2021

### Other
>Demersal otter trawling is responsible for 25% of the world's discarded bycatch.

-Kennelly 2021

>The "zones" of an otter trawl are: (1) spreading mechanisms,  (2) entry point, (3) trawl body (sequentially smaller meshes), (4) extension and codend.

-Kennelly 2021

>74% of research on gear modifications has happened in Europe. Only 17% of papers came from North America.

-Kennelly 2021

>7% of experiments in zone 1, 11% in zone 2, 11% in zone 3, with a whopping 71% in zone 4.

-Kennelly 2021

>Clearly more research should be done to extend things to other species but likely  a great deal of the work done so far can be reused.

-Kennelly 2021

>There was no clear trend of more complex modifications being better than simpler ones. So starting simple and in the codend is likely best.

-Kennelly 2021

## Salmo

-Olafsson 2016

>Genetic database for Atlantic salmon: SALSEA-Merge project. 26,813 fish from 13 European countries, 284 rivers, and 466 sample sites. Represents ~85% of the non-Baltic European salmon population.

-Olafsson 2016

>Assignment of fish by Bayesian assignment method implemented  in GENECLASS2.

-Olafsson 2016

### Life history
>Atlantic salmon spawn in freshwater, have a juvenile phase of 1-8 years, and subsequent 1-4 year oceanic feeding migrations.

-Olafsson 2016

>Most fish (134 of 186, 73%) were in their first year at sea.  Freshwater age ranged from 1 to 5 years (mean 2.6).

-Olafsson 2016

>A general trend of increasing smolt age with increasing latitude has been  observed.

-Olafsson 2016

### Other

-Olafsson 2016

>Post smolts have been caught north of Scotland, in the Norwegian  sea, and along Labrador.

-Olafsson 2016

>Salmon from North America generally remain in the western North Atlantic although migration to Europe has occurred. Feeding areas north of the Faroe Islands are used by northern European salmon.  Southern European fish are also abundant off West Greenland.

-Olafsson 2016
